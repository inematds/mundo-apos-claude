🏛️ A Jarvis architecture

The parts that make up a personal AI assistant and how they fit together.

What it is:

An AI assistant that knows you, remembers things, and handles tasks for you — like Iron Man’s Jarvis.

Why learn:

That’s the destination of the course: your own assistant.

Key concepts:

Personal · knows you · gets things done.

What it is:

Brain (the AI), memory (what it remembers), skills (what it can do), and triggers (what activates it).

Why learn:

Understanding the pieces is the map to building your own.

Key concepts:

Brain · memory · skills · triggers.

What it is:

The trigger activates the brain, which consults memory and uses skills to act.

Why learn:

Seeing the workflow keeps you from building a Frankenstein.

Key concepts:

Flow · fit · coordination.

What it is:

Your Jarvis can run on your computer (locally) or on servers (in the cloud)—each has pros and cons.

Why learn:

The choice affects cost, privacy, and ease of use.

Key concepts:

Local · cloud · trade-off.

What it is:

Decide what your Jarvis can see and store, while keeping control in your hands.

Why learn:

A personal assistant handles your stuff — privacy matters.

Key concepts:

Privacy · control · limits.

What it is:

Draft what your assistant will do before building it—start with the goal.

Why learn:

A simple plan prevents rework.

Key concepts:

Plan · goal · draft.

🔭 OpenClaw, Hermes, and agentic systems

An overview of ready-made Jarvis systems — including INEMA courses — and how to choose yours.

What it is:

Prebuilt Jarvis kits where you configure instead of building from scratch.

Why learn:

It could save you months of work.

Key concepts:

Ready · configure · shortcut.

What it is:

An open AI family (Nous Research) used to build custom assistants. INEMA has courses dedicated to it.

Why learn:

It’s one of the most widely used foundations for a self-hosted Jarvis.

Key concepts:

Open · self-hosted · foundational.

What it is:

A multi-channel autonomous AI assistant (runs via Docker). There’s also a dedicated INEMA course.

Why learn:

A good path to a Jarvis that serves multiple channels.

Key concepts:

Autonomous · multi-channel · Docker.

What it is:

There are plenty of other projects and tools—a general overview so you don’t feel lost.

Why learn:

Map the territory before choosing.

Key concepts:

Options · overview · big picture.

What it is:

Choose based on your needs: privacy, channels, ease of use, cost.

Why learn:

There’s no “best”—there’s what’s best for you.

Key concepts:

Need · criteria · adjustment.

What it is:

Choose ONE path and follow the corresponding INEMA course, without jumping from one thing to another.

Why learn:

Focus beats choice paralysis.

Key concepts:

Choose one · follow through · stay focused.

A sequence of automatic steps: do A, then B, then C.

Why learn:

Workflows handle complete jobs, not just little tasks.

Key concepts:

Sequence · steps · linking.

What it is:

Specialized agents already configured (researcher, reviewer, writer) for you to reuse.

Why learn:

A ready-made team, without building each one from scratch.

Key concepts:

Specialized · template · team.

What it is:

Search for sources → verify → automatically summarize in a report.

Why learn:

A complete example to copy.

Key concepts:

Search · verify · summarize.

What it is:

Idea → script → text → image, linked together in a single workflow.

Why learn:

Personal content production line.

Key concepts:

Idea · script · production.

Key concepts:

Skeleton · swap · adjust.

What it is:

Add checkpoints between steps so nothing goes off track.

Why learn:

⚡ Automation and triggers

The Jarvis that acts on its own: routines that run without you pressing anything.

What it is:

The three ways to activate Jarvis: a schedule, something that happens, or a command from you.

Why learn:

A trigger is what turns on “proactive mode.”

Key concepts:

Time · event · command.

What it is:

Program the assistant to do things at set times (e.g., a summary every morning).

Why learn:

That’s when Jarvis starts working without you asking.

Key concepts:

Schedule · routine · proactive.

What it is:

Talk to your Jarvis through Telegram or email, from anywhere.

Why learn:

Keeps the assistant just a tap away.

Key concepts:

Telegram · email · access.

What it is:

Tasks that run overnight and leave everything ready for you in the morning.

Why learn:

Your time pays off even when you’re not there.

Key concepts:

Overnight · ready · render.

What it is:

Track what Jarvis did and get alerts when something goes off track.

Why learn:

Autonomy with your eyes open, not in the dark.

Key concepts:

Track · alerts · control.

What it is:

News digest, smart reminder, email triage — ready-to-use ideas.

Why learn:

Move on from “what should I automate?” with a list.

Key concepts:

Summary · reminder · triage.
